# 0.4.x

## 0.4.2 — 2026-10-09

### Fixed

- **The green speaking border disappeared around participant tiles.** The local preview was positioned
  absolutely with no positioned ancestor, so it escaped the voice panel and covered the tiles beneath
  it. The preview now sits in the panel's own flow, where it cannot overlap anything.
- A participant's camera no longer vanishes when the room re-renders. Tiles are rebuilt from scratch on
  every state change, which detached the video attached to them; live video is now re-placed after each
  render.

### Changed

- **A participant's camera replaces their profile box.** Their tile shows the live video instead of the
  avatar, with the name and connection state underneath. The avatar is the fallback for when no camera
  is running, and is never shown at the same time as the video.
- **A screen share gets its own box**, separate from the voice and camera tiles, above the participant
  list. A share is content rather than a person, so it is presented at full width and letterboxed rather
  than being squeezed into a tile. A participant who is sharing still gets a mark on their tile.
- The camera marks that used to sit on every tile are gone: a running camera is now shown by the video
  itself, so a mark next to it was only noise.

## 0.4.1 — 2026-10-09

### Fixed

- **The camera could not be turned on inside the app.** The page asked the WebView for the camera
  directly, but an Android WebView will not open a camera the app itself has not been granted, so the
  request was refused with nothing to show for it. The native permission is now taken in context, just
  before the camera starts, and a refusal explains itself: a permanent one says to turn it on in system
  settings rather than inviting another prompt that cannot appear.
- The native permission bridge captured the platform plugin once, when the page's modules were first
  evaluated. Capacitor registers plugins later than that, so on a slow start the bridge reported itself
  unavailable for the life of the page and every permission request silently did nothing. It now
  resolves the plugin on each use.
- What you are publishing is previewed locally while it runs, so a misframed camera or the wrong screen
  is caught before the room sees it. The preview is muted and never published itself, a front camera is
  mirrored when the platform says it is one, and it is removed as soon as the source stops.

### Added

- A persistent banner while your camera or screen is live, naming what is being sent and offering to
  stop it, instead of relying on a highlighted button that is easy to forget.

## 0.4.0 — 2026-10-09

Camera and screen sharing in voice rooms, on the same encrypted connection as the microphone.

### Added

- **Camera in voice rooms.** A camera control turns your camera on and off, and a second control switches between available cameras. Camera permission is requested only when you turn the camera on; joining a room never starts it. Your camera is released as soon as you turn it off, leave the room, or lose access to it.
- **Screen sharing where the platform supports it.** A share control opens the browser's own picker and publishes what you choose. Cancelling the picker leaves the room exactly as it was. On a platform with no picker — including the Android WebView — the control stays visible, disabled, and explains that screen sharing is not available there rather than failing after you have committed to it.
- **Remote video and screen shares** appear above the participant list while anyone is publishing. A shared screen gets the full width and is letterboxed, because cropping text is worse than letterboxing it; a camera tile is centred and cropped the way a camera normally is. Participant tiles show a camera or share mark for whoever actually has one running, and nothing is shown for a camera or share that is not there.
- Screen capture is tuned for legibility rather than framerate, because a shared screen is mostly text.

### Security

- Camera and screen video are published on the same encrypted LiveKit connection as the microphone, using the same key. Nothing is published before encryption is confirmed, and there is no unencrypted fallback: if the encrypted connection is not ready, the request is refused and reported rather than quietly sending in the clear.
- Capture is opt-in per source. Camera and screen share are independent controls, so sharing a screen does not turn on a camera, and neither starts on joining or rejoining.
- Remote video elements are detached and every capture released when the room ends, access is revoked, or the device moves to another device, so a camera cannot stay open after the call that justified it.

### Changed

- Voice room participants report whether they are publishing a camera or a screen share, so a participant's state is visible in the room rather than only in their own window.

### Known gaps

- **Android screen sharing is not implemented.** The Android System WebView has no screen-capture picker, and capturing the screen natively requires MediaProjection and a foreground service. Until that exists the control explains itself instead of pretending. Camera works on Android.
- Screen sharing is one screen at a time per participant and does not carry system audio. Android restricts which apps may be captured for playback, so audio is not promised until it is tested.
- iOS screen sharing needs a ReplayKit broadcast extension and a macOS build; it is a separate follow-up.
- Receiving video is only shown in the voice room panel. Direct calls keep their existing audio-only presentation.
